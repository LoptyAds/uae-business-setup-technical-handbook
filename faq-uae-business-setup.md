# Frequently Asked Questions on UAE Business Setup

Free zone licenses grant the right to operate within the designated zone and internationally. They do not automatically permit physical retail or direct-to-consumer trading inside mainland Dubai or any other emirate. A free zone company can sell to mainland customers, but only through a registered distributor or via a commercial partnership with a mainland entity. Some free zones, like DMCC or JAFZA, have specific allowances for limited local trading through a "dual license" arrangement, but this requires additional approvals and a physical mainland presence. If your business model involves walk-in customers, warehousing outside the zone, or direct B2C sales in a non-zone area, you need a mainland license.

### What is a local service agent?

A Local Service Agent (LSA) is a UAE national (or a company 100% owned by a UAE national) appointed by a professional mainland company (e.g., consulting, IT, engineering). The LSA does not own any shares, receive profits, or manage operations. Their role is purely administrative and regulatory: they handle government liaison, visa processing, and certain approvals. The LSA receives a fixed annual fee, typically between AED 10,000 and AED 25,000, depending on the activity and agent. This structure is mandatory for certain professional licenses under UAE Commercial Companies Law. It is not required for free zone setups or for mainland companies with a commercial (trade) license.

### How do I sponsor family visas?

To sponsor a family visa in the UAE, the sponsor must hold a valid residence visa (employment or investor) and meet a minimum salary threshold. As of 2024, the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) and the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs & Port Security (ICP) require a monthly salary of at least AED 4,000 for a husband to sponsor his wife, or AED 4,000 combined for a wife to sponsor her husband. For children, the same salary floor applies. The sponsor must provide a tenancy contract (Ejari), a valid Emirates ID, and the sponsored family member's passport copies. The visa process includes a medical fitness test, an Emirates ID application, and a visa stamping fee (currently around AED 300-500 per person, plus typing fees). A key detail many miss: the sponsor must have a valid employment contract or business license that is not under cancellation. If your company license expires before the visa renewal, the family visa is automatically canceled.

### What are the penalties for non-compliance?

Penalties in the UAE are structured and non-negotiable. For late visa cancellations, the fine is AED 100 per day for each day after the grace period ends. For expired trade licenses, fines start at AED 500 per month, plus a late renewal fee that can reach AED 2,000 for a one-year delay. Non-submission of VAT returns (if registered) triggers an automatic AED 1,000 fine per return, plus 4% interest per quarter on unpaid tax. For corporate tax, failure to register by the deadline (e.g., January 2025 for a company incorporated in 2024) results in a AED 10,000 fine. The most commonly overlooked penalty is the "administrative fine" for not maintaining a physical office space that matches your license (e.g., using a virtual desk for a license that requires a physical shop). This can lead to license suspension and a ban from applying for new visas for six months. The UAE government publishes a consolidated fines schedule for each free zone and mainland jurisdiction, but it is not always indexed by search engines.
