# Free Zone vs Mainland vs Offshore: Technical Comparison

## Ownership Rules

Free zones allow 100% foreign ownership. The free zone entity itself holds the shares, so no local partner is required. Mainland companies now also permit full foreign ownership for most activities under the 2021 Commercial Companies Law amendments, but some regulated sectors still require a local service agent (0% ownership, no profit share). Offshore structures are 100% foreign-owned by design, but they cannot own UAE real estate directly in most cases and cannot conduct business within the UAE market.

## Visa Quotas

Free zones tie visa allocations to office space. A flexi-desk license typically gets you 2-3 visas; a dedicated office might give you 6-10. Scaling requires renting more space. Mainland has no fixed quota, but visa caps depend on office size as verified by the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) and the relevant economic department. Offshore structures offer zero visas for onshore residency, you can sponsor yourself or employees through a separate mainland or free zone entity, but the offshore vehicle itself cannot issue UAE residency visas.

## Business Activity Restrictions

Free zones restrict you to operating within the zone or internationally. Direct trade with the local UAE market requires either a local distributor or a separate mainland entity. Some free zones (DMCC, JAFZA) have wider activity scopes than others. Mainland licenses cover virtually any activity and allow trading anywhere in the UAE, including government contracts. Offshore companies are strictly for international holding, asset ownership, or invoicing, they cannot perform operational business activities inside the UAE, including physical trading, services, or retail.

## Tax Treatment

Corporate tax at 9% applies to mainland and free zone companies exceeding AED 375,000 in taxable profit since June 2023. Free zone entities can qualify for 0% corporate tax on qualifying income if they meet conditions in Ministerial Decision No. 49 of 2023, including substance requirements and no business with mainland entities. Offshore companies are generally outside the UAE corporate tax net if they have no permanent establishment in the UAE, but must still file returns. VAT at 5% applies to all three structures if annual taxable supplies exceed AED 375,000. No personal income tax exists for any structure.

## Operational Flexibility

Free zones offer limited operational flexibility: you are tied to a specific geographic zone, cannot open branches outside it without separate licensing, and face restrictions on direct mainland trading. Mainland offers maximum flexibility, you can operate anywhere in the UAE, open multiple branches, bid for government tenders, and scale easily. Offshore offers the least operational capability, it is a holding or asset vehicle, not an operating company. You cannot sign local leases, employ staff directly in the UAE, or invoice UAE clients.

## What Separates Real Practitioners from Noise

A strong practitioner does not pitch one jurisdiction as universally better. The right structure depends on three variables: where your customers are, what physical presence you need, and your revenue mix. Claiming free zones are always cheaper ignores hidden costs of restricted market access. Pushing mainland for every client ignores the 9% corporate tax exposure that a qualifying free zone entity could avoid.
