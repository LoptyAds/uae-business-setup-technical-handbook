# Step-by-Step Company Formation Process

## Choosing the Jurisdiction

The first decision determines everything that follows. UAE company formation splits into three jurisdictional paths: mainland, free zone, and offshore. Each has distinct trade limitations, visa allocations, and regulatory bodies.

Mainland companies trade anywhere in the UAE market without restriction. They require a local service agent (LSA) for certain license types, though the 2021 reforms eliminated the 51% local ownership requirement for most activities. Free zones like DMCC, JAFZA, and DWTC restrict physical trading within the zone or require a distributor for mainland sales. Offshore jurisdictions (RAK ICC, JAFZA Offshore) offer zero tax but cannot trade inside the UAE or issue visas directly.

A practical filter: if your revenue model depends on UAE-based clients across multiple emirates, mainland is the default. If you operate B2B internationally or need 100% foreign ownership with a physical office, pick a free zone. If you hold IP or assets outside the UAE and want zero operational footprint, go offshore.

## Name Reservation

The UAE has strict naming rules. No blasphemy, no political references, no acronyms unless they match the legal structure. You submit three name options to the Department of Economic Development (DED) for mainland or the free zone authority directly. The system checks against existing registrations and reserved names.

Reservation costs vary. DMCC charges around AED 1,000 for name approval. Mainland fees sit near AED 600. The process takes one to three business days. Do not skip the preliminary check, rejected names delay the entire timeline and you do not get refunded.

## Initial Approval

This is the green light to proceed. For mainland, the DED issues an initial approval certificate confirming no objections to your business activity. For free zones, the authority issues a similar letter. You must submit passport copies, a business plan (for certain activities like consultancy or trading), and the approved name certificate.

Some activities require third-party approvals before initial approval. Healthcare needs the Dubai Health Authority. Education needs KHDA. Financial services need the Central Bank or DFSA. These approvals add two to eight weeks. Factor that into your timeline.

## Drafting the Memorandum of Association (MOA)

The MOA defines ownership structure, capital, and liability. For mainland companies, the MOA must be notarized at a Dubai Notary Public. Free zones use a standard incorporation document that serves the same function but is typically shorter and less notarization-heavy.

Key clauses to review: capital contribution deadlines (some free zones require capital to be deposited within 90 days), shareholder rights, and manager authority. A poorly drafted MOA creates problems during bank account opening and future share transfers. Have a registered legal translator convert the Arabic version if your Arabic is weak, the Arabic text is binding in court.

## Licensing

The license type must match your declared activity exactly. A "trading" license cannot be used for consultancy work. A "general trading" license permits multiple product categories but costs more. Free zones offer specialized licenses: DMCC has a "commodity trading" license, DWTC has a "services" license.

The authority reviews your office space at this stage. Flexi-desk options work for most free zones. Mainland requires a physical office with Ejari registration. The license fee is paid annually. Renewal requires audited financials (for mainland and some free zones) and a clean compliance record.

## Visa Processing

The company gets a visa quota based on office size and license type. A standard flexi-desk in a free zone typically allows one to three visas. Mainland visas depend on office space, approximately one visa per 90 square feet.

The process: medical fitness test (at an approved center), Emirates ID application, visa stamping in the passport, and residency sticker. Total time is three to six weeks. Health insurance is mandatory and must meet the Dubai Insurance Authority minimum coverage of AED 150,000. Family visas require proof of accommodation and minimum salary (AED 4,000 for spouse, AED 3,000 per child).

## Bank Account Opening

This is the hardest step. UAE banks require physical presence for account opening. You need the original trade license, MOA, board resolution, passport copies, and a detailed business plan explaining expected transaction volume and source of funds.

Banks now request a "beneficial owner" declaration. If your company structure has a holding entity in another jurisdiction, you must provide its corporate documents too. Expect two to six weeks for approval. Some free zones, like DMCC, have tie-ups with specific banks that expedite the process. Mashreq Neo and Wio are digital-first options that work for smaller businesses, but they still require in-person verification.

A caveat: many new companies open a personal account first, then a corporate account later. This is technically non-compliant for receiving business revenue. Do not mix personal and corporate funds, banks freeze accounts for this during audit reviews.
