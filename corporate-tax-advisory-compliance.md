# Corporate Tax Advisory and Compliance Essentials

## Corporate Tax and VAT: The Real Compliance Baseline

UAE corporate tax applies at 9% on taxable income exceeding AED 375,000. Below that threshold, the rate is 0%. This is not a progressive bracket system, it is a flat 9% on the excess only. A business earning AED 500,000 in taxable profit pays 9% on AED 125,000, which comes to AED 11,250. New setups often misinterpret this as a 9% rate on all profit. That mistake leads to over-provisioning cash reserves or, worse, underpayment if they miscalculate the base.

Free zone qualifying income rules are where most practitioners separate themselves from copy-paste advisors. A free zone entity must meet the "qualifying activities" test and maintain adequate substance in the UAE to benefit from the 0% rate on qualifying income. The list of qualifying activities includes manufacturing, logistics, warehousing, and certain financial services, but not passive investment or retail sales to the mainland. If a free zone company sells goods directly to a mainland customer without a mainland distributor or branch, that income may be reclassified as non-qualifying and taxed at 9%. ### VAT Registration Thresholds

VAT registration is mandatory when taxable supplies and imports exceed AED 375,000 over the previous 12 months. Voluntary registration is possible at AED 187,500. The mandatory threshold matches the corporate tax threshold, which creates confusion, they are separate calculations with separate reporting obligations. A consultancy earning AED 200,000 in fees with AED 50,000 in expenses does not need to register for VAT but still files a corporate tax return if profit exceeds the threshold. The reverse also happens: a trading company with AED 400,000 in revenue but thin margins may owe no corporate tax but must register for VAT.

### Record-Keeping Requirements

The Federal Tax Authority requires records to be kept for five years from the end of the relevant tax period. For corporate tax, that period is the financial year. For VAT, it is the calendar quarter or month depending on filing frequency. Records must include all invoices, contracts, bank statements, and underlying source documents. Digital records are acceptable, but the FTA can request physical copies during an audit. Dubai-based businesses that treat record-keeping as a bookkeeping task rather than a compliance function risk a penalty reassessment from a missing invoice two years old, even if the tax was correctly paid. The penalty for failing to maintain records starts at AED 10,000 for the first violation and escalates.

One honest caveat: the free zone 0% tax regime is still being tested in practice. The Ministry of Finance has not issued exhaustive guidance on every borderline case, for example, a free zone company that provides consultancy services to a mainland client while operating entirely from its free zone office. Some practitioners argue the income qualifies if the service is performed within the free zone. Others take a stricter view. Until a formal clarification or a tax tribunal ruling emerges, there is genuine uncertainty. A competent advisor acknowledges this rather than promising a guaranteed 0% outcome.
